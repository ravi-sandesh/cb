# Chowka Bara (7x7) – Rules & Examples

**Chowka Bara** (also known as *Ashta Chamma*, *Katte Mane*, *Chakaara*, or *Thaayam*) is an ancient Indian race board game. While the 5x5 version is highly popular, the **7x7 variant (7-House)** is widely considered the "pro" version, involving a longer path, more safe zones, and deeper strategy.

---

## 1. Components Required
*   **The Board:** A 7x7 grid (49 squares in total). 
*   **Pawns (Goti):** 2 to 4 players. Each player has **4 pawns** of a distinct color.
*   **Dice:** Traditionally played with **4 cowrie shells** (Kavade). You can also use traditional stick dice or binary coins.

---

## 2. Cowrie Shell Values (The Dice)
When the 4 cowrie shells are thrown, the number of "mouths" facing up determines the move value:

*   **1 mouth up:** Move 1 square.
*   **2 mouths up:** Move 2 squares.
*   **3 mouths up:** Move 3 squares.
*   **4 mouths up (Chowka):** Move 4 squares **+ Bonus Roll**.
*   **0 mouths up (Ashta / all inverted):** Move 8 squares **+ Bonus Roll**.

*Note: If a player rolls a 4 or an 8, they continue to roll until they get a 1, 2, or 3. They then divide the accumulated moves among their pawns.*

---

## 3. Board Layout & Safe Zones
The board is a 7x7 grid. The path starts from the outer edge, spirals inward in concentric rings, and ends at the exact center square.

**Safe Zones (Crossed Squares):**
Certain squares on the board are marked with an "X". Pawns in these squares **cannot be captured (killed)**. 
A standard 7x7 board usually contains **9 Safe Squares**:
*   The **4 starting squares** (the middle square on each of the four outer edges).
*   The **4 inner corners** of the second ring.
*   The **Central Square** (The destination).

Multiple pawns (even from different players) can coexist peacefully on a safe square.

---

## 4. Basic Rules & Gameplay

### A. Starting the Game
*   Players choose a starting square on their side of the board. All 4 pawns begin off the board or in the starting safe square.
*   Unlike some games (like Ludo), pawns usually don't need a specific number to enter the board. Any roll can be used to move a pawn out of the home square.

### B. Movement
*   **Direction:** Pawns move **anti-clockwise** in the outermost ring. Once they complete the outer circuit and return to their starting side, they step into the next inner ring and move **clockwise**. They spiral inwards until they reach the center.
*   **Splitting Moves:** If a player earns multiple rolls (e.g., rolling an 8, then a 4, then a 2), they can assign the '8' to one pawn, the '4' to another, and the '2' to a third. They can also combine them to move a single pawn 14 squares.

### C. Hitting (Capturing) 
*   If a player's pawn lands on a square occupied by an opponent's single pawn, the opponent's pawn is **"killed" (captured)** and sent back to its starting square.
*   **Extra Turn:** A player gets an immediate bonus roll for killing an opponent's pawn.
*   **Inner Ring Access (The Toll/Chit rule):** A player **cannot** enter the inner rings of the board unless they have captured at least one opponent's pawn during the game. If they reach the entrance to the inner ring but haven't captured anyone, their pawns get stuck or must continue looping the outer ring (depending on regional rules).

### D. Doubles / Pairs (Jugga)
*   If a player lands two of their own pawns on the same square, they form a **"Double"**.
*   A Double acts as a blockade. An opponent's single pawn cannot jump over or kill a Double. 
*   A Double can only be killed by an opponent's Double. 
*   To move a Double, the player must roll an even number (2, 4, or 8) and moves the pair half the distance (e.g., rolling a 4 moves the Double 2 spaces). 

### E. Winning the Game
*   Pawns must enter the **Central Square** by an **exact roll**. (e.g., if you are 2 steps away from the center, you must roll exactly a 2 or two 1s).
*   The first player to get all 4 pawns safely into the central square wins the game!

---

## 5. Gameplay Examples

**Example 1: The Bonus Roll Sequence**
*   *Player A* rolls an 8 (Ashta). They get a bonus roll.
*   *Player A* rolls a 4 (Chowka). They get another bonus roll.
*   *Player A* rolls a 3. The turn ends.
*   *Move choice:* Player A can move Pawn-1 by 8 squares, Pawn-2 by 4 squares, and Pawn-3 by 3 squares. Alternatively, they can move Pawn-1 by 15 squares (8+4+3).

**Example 2: Capturing for Inner Access**
*   *Player B* rolls a 2. Their pawn lands on a normal square occupied by *Player C's* pawn.
*   *Result:* Player C's pawn goes back to the start. Player B gets an extra roll. 
*   *Strategic Benefit:* Player B has now fulfilled the "Capture" condition and their pawns are officially unlocked to enter the inner 5x5 and 3x3 rings when they complete the outer circuit.

**Example 3: Exact Roll for the Center**
*   *Player D* has one pawn left. It is 3 squares away from the central destination.
*   *Player D* rolls a 4. 
*   *Result:* Since an exact roll of 3 (or a combination like 2+1) is required, Player D cannot move that pawn. If they have no other pawns to move, they forfeit the turn.
